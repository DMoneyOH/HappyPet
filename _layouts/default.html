{%- comment -%} Round 4. These were eight invented labels ("Beds & crates", "Scratchers & trees") all pointing at /dogs/ or /cats/ — shelf headings for a catalogue that does not exist, which is why they read as arbitrary. Every label below is a real category in _posts front matter with published reviews behind it, and each one is also the top category for its species by post count: dog-health 8, dog-toys 4, dog-beds 3, dog-travel 3, cat-litter 3, cat-food 3, cat-toys 2, cat-scratching 2. The full VALID_CATEGORIES list in refill_products.py has 25 entries; most are aspirational and several have one post or none, so it is the wrong source for a footer. ROUND 5: the label is NO LONGER the query, and that separation is the point. The labels used to repeat their own heading — "Dog health" under "For dogs" — so the species was printed twice in every line. The visible text is the bare category now; the heading supplies the species. The hrefs are UNCHANGED and must stay that way. /search/ reads ?q= and its filter requires every 3+ character term to appear in a post's title or tags, and detectSpecies pre-filters off-species posts, so the species word in the query is load-bearing even though it is no longer on screen. Dropping it from a href — "?q=health" — would return cross-species results or none. Each of these eight queries was checked against the real front matter in round 4 and returns at least two reviews; changing a query without re-running that check will quietly break it. "Toys" appearing in both columns is correct, not a duplicate to tidy: the two links go to different queries. {%- endcomment -%}
