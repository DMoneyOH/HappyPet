The right one keeps a cat working for kibble instead of begging for it, and it can ease the vomiting that comes from scarfing food too fast. ## Buying guide

Match the puzzle to the cat, not the other way around. A food-obsessed cat bulldozes through an easy feeder and needs a harder design to stay challenged, while a timid or senior cat quits fast when the openings feel like work. Start easy and raise the difficulty as your cat gains confidence, and drop back a level if she walks away hungry for a couple of days.

Think about the food you plan to use. Dry kibble rolls and drops cleanly through most puzzle feeders, but wet food gums up the channels and turns cleaning into a chore. If your cat eats mostly wet food, a puzzle feeder works better for treats and dry snacks than for the main meal.

Cleaning decides whether a feeder stays in the rotation or ends up in a cupboard. Units with deep narrow channels trap crumbs and oils, so look for one you can rinse quickly or drop in the dishwasher when daily washing sounds like too much. The best cat puzzle feeder is the one your cat actually uses, and a grimy feeder you dread cleaning gets used less.

Consider the floor and the cat's habits. A light feeder slides when a strong cat paws at it, so a non-slip base or a placemat underneath keeps the game in one spot. A puzzle feeder handles the boredom and the speed, not the schedule, so pair it with an [automatic cat feeder](https://happypetproductreviews.com/cat-feeders/best-automatic-cat-feeder/) if you also need timed meals while you are out of the house.
