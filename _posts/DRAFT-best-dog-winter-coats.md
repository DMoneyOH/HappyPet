## Buying guide

Start with whether your dog needs a coat at all. Thick double-coated breeds handle cold well on their own. Short-haired and older dogs lose heat faster, and they benefit most from a dog winter coat on cold walks.

Next, think about weather. Dry cold calls for warmth. Wet cold calls for a waterproof shell, because damp fur pulls heat away from your dog's body fast. If your winters bring more slush than snow, put waterproofing at the top of your list.

Fit matters more than any feature. Measure from the base of the neck to the base of the tail, then around the widest part of the chest. A good coat covers the back and chest without pinching behind the front legs. Too loose and it slides around; too tight and your dog walks stiffly.

Check closures and the leash setup. A leash hole lets you keep a harness on under the coat, which saves a lot of fumbling with cold fingers. Easy fasteners help if your dog squirms.

Plan for washing. Winter walks mean mud and road salt, so a coat you can wipe or wash keeps it in use all season. A reversible coat gives you a backup side while the dirty one dries. If your dog swims in warmer months, our [dog life jacket](https://happypetproductreviews.com/outdoor/best-dog-life-jacket/) roundup covers water safety gear.

Watch your dog on the first few outings. Shivering or a dog who turns back toward the house signals the coat isn't doing enough, or that the walk needs to be shorter.

## Does a short-haired dog need a winter coat?

A dog winter coat helps most for short-haired breeds and senior dogs, since they lose body heat faster in cold weather. Thick double-coated breeds usually stay warm without one. If your dog shivers or tries to head home early on cold walks, a coat is worth trying. Ask your vet if your dog has a health condition that affects heat.

## How do you measure a dog for a winter coat?

Measure your dog's back from the base of the neck to the base of the tail, then measure around the widest part of the chest just behind the front legs. Compare both numbers to the size chart on the coat's listing. Chest size often matters more than length, since a tight chest restricts movement. If your dog falls between sizes, pick the larger one.

A waterproof dog coat makes sense if your winters bring sleet or wet snow, because wet fur loses heat much faster than dry fur.
