## Buying guide

Start with the brush. Fur on hard floors sits loose and lifts with plain suction. Fur on upholstery works into the fabric, and suction by itself leaves a lot of it behind. A motorized brush loosens that hair first, which matters more than any suction claim printed on a box.

Next, pick cordless or corded. A cordless model goes anywhere, including the car at the far end of the driveway. You trade some freedom for battery limits, so check the runtime on the listing and keep sessions short. A corded unit never quits mid-job, but the cord decides where you can clean.

Look at the dust cup and filter before you order. Pet hair takes up room in a small bin, so check how the cup empties. Check whether the filter rinses clean or needs a replacement, and clear it often.

Match the tool to the job. If your dog rides on fabric seats or your cat sheds on a textured couch, the brush matters most. Smooth surfaces wipe clean with less effort, so a basic handheld covers them.

## How often should you vacuum pet hair off furniture?

During heavy shedding, a quick handheld pass every few days keeps pet hair from sinking deep into couch fabric. Short, frequent sessions beat one long scrub after the fur has packed in. Outside shedding season, once a week usually covers it. Brushing your dog or cat outside also cuts how much hair lands on the furniture at all.
