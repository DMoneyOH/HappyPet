## Buying guide

Start with flavor when you compare the best lickable cat treats. Cats form strong opinions about protein, so a variety box lets you test before you commit to a single flavor in bulk. Once you know what your cat eats, a box built around that protein wastes fewer tubes.

Next, think about texture. Lickable treats run soft, so they suit cats who struggle with hard biscuits. If you plan to use them as a topper, a softer, wetter treat spreads over kibble more easily.

Count matters for budget. A bigger box lowers what you pay per tube, but only if your cat finishes it. Our rule: never buy a single-flavor bulk box for a cat you have not tested on that flavor. A variety box costs less than a cupboard full of tubes your cat refuses.

Treat calories still count. Lickable treats feel light, but they add up across a week. Keep them to a small share of your cat's daily food, and trim the regular meal a little if your cat carries extra weight. Ask your vet before you add treats for a cat on a prescription diet or with kidney trouble.

Storage helps too. Keep the box closed and out of reach, since a determined cat can chew through a thin wrapper. ## Can lickable cat treats help with giving pills?

Lickable cat treats can make pill time easier, since a soft treat gives you something to hide a tablet in and something your cat wants to lick. Offer a plain treat first, then one with the pill mixed in, while your cat feels hungry before a meal. Ask your vet first, because some pills should not be crushed or taken with food.

## How many lickable treats can a cat have per day?

Lickable treats should stay a small part of a cat's daily food. The right number depends on your cat's weight and regular diet, so follow the feeding guide printed on the package as a starting point. An overweight cat needs a smaller share, and a cat on a prescription diet needs a daily limit from your vet before any treats.
