## Buying guide

Start with the format. A tin gives you more balm for the money and lets you work it into deep cracks with a fingertip. A stick keeps your hands clean and speeds up the routine, which matters when you have a dog who hates foot handling. Pick the one you will use every day, since a balm only works when you apply it.

Check the ingredient panel next. Dogs lick their feet, so whatever goes on the pads ends up in the mouth. Look for a short list built on butters and oils, and skip anything with added fragrance you can't identify. If your dog has a history of allergies, ask your vet before you try a new balm.

Think about timing. The best dog paw balm still fails if you apply it right before your dog trots across wet grass. Rub it in after a walk, once the pads are dry, or before bedtime so it has hours to soak in. On salted streets, wipe the paws with a damp cloth first to clear grit and salt.

Know when a balm isn't enough. Deep cracks that bleed or pads that look swollen point to something a balm can't fix. Ask your vet if a crack doesn't improve within a few days. ## How often should you put paw balm on a dog?

Dog paw balm works best as a daily habit during winter. Apply a thin layer once a day, ideally after the evening walk once the pads are dry, and add a second coat on days with heavy road salt. A 3.5 oz tin such as the Palmers cocoa butter balm holds enough for a steady daily routine.

## Can a dog lick off paw balm?

A dog can lick off paw balm, and most will try at least once. Rub a thin layer in until it absorbs, then distract your dog with a chew or a short play session for a few minutes. The Palmers formula leans on cocoa butter, so watch for licking and ask your vet if your dog eats a lot of it.
