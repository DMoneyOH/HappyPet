## What to look for in a travel water bottle

A few features separate a bottle you actually carry from one that lives in a drawer. Capacity sets the first limit. A 19oz bottle covers a medium dog on an hour-long walk, while a large dog on a summer hike can empty that in a single stop, so match the size to your dog and your route. Leak protection matters just as much, because a bottle that seeps inside a bag soaks everything it touches. Look for a lock button or a threaded cap you can trust, then test it over the kitchen sink before you trust it on the road.

Weight decides whether the thing leaves the house at all. A heavy stainless bottle keeps water cool but drags on a daily walk, while a light plastic one clips on and gets forgotten in the good way. Cleaning counts too. A wide opening lets you rinse and dry the trough so it does not grow a film after a few weeks. The squeeze-and-drink style beats a folding bowl for one plain reason: the water your dog leaves behind flows back into the bottle instead of onto the pavement. That saves a few ounces on a long day and skips the puddle you would otherwise leave on somebody's lawn.
