## Buying Guide

Five things to check on any bag, in the order we'd check them.

### 1. Clumping Ability

This is the one we'd never compromise on. Clumps that hold together come out whole, so less used litter stays in the box giving off smell. Look for "hard clumping" or "strong clumping" on the packaging. Pair a hard-clumping litter with a box that is easy to scoop: our [litter box guide](/litter/best-cat-litter-boxes/) compares boxes built for easy cleaning and odor control.

### 2. Litter Type and Material

Clay litters absorb moisture and clump. Silica gel litters are absorbent, and brands often say they last longer between changes. We don't rank one material over another for odor. All three picks here are clumping litters, and Dr. Elsey's is clay. If your cat already uses its box happily, weigh that before you change the texture under its paws.

### 3. Scented vs. Unscented

In this guide, scent is an optional extra and clumping does the main work. If you like a scented litter and your cat uses the box without fuss, keep it. If your cat seems hesitant after you switch to a scented litter, or fragrance bothers you or someone in the house, go unscented. Dr. Elsey's Ultra Unscented is the pick here.

### 4. Dust Level

Dust is the part you notice while pouring and scooping. If it bothers you, look for "low dust" on the bag, and read it as the maker's claim. For the rest of the setup, see our pick for a sturdy [cat scratching post](/scratching/best-cat-scratching-posts/).

### 5. Multi-Cat Formulas

If more than one cat shares a box, we'd start with a multi-cat formula. That's the use they're sold for.
