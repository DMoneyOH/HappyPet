## Buying guide

Start with the stand. A heavy, wide stand matters more than any accessory, because it keeps the tree upright if your cat does make a run at it. For extra security, tie the top of the tree to a small wall hook with clear fishing line.

Next, look at what draws your cat in. Ornaments that swing and catch the light at paw height invite batting, so hang shatterproof ones on the bottom third and move glass ones up high. Skip tinsel. A cat that swallows a strand can end up at the emergency clinic, so ask your vet what to watch for if it happens.

Give your cat a legal place to climb. A tunnel skirt at the base or a cat tree a few feet away gives your cat somewhere to perch and hide. Toss a treat into that spot each time your cat uses it, and it starts to feel like the better deal.

Cover the tree water. Pine sap and any additive in the stand water don't belong in your cat's stomach, so tuck a skirt or a sheet of foil over the bowl.

Expect to combine a few of these steps. One fix alone rarely keeps your cat out of the Christmas tree for the whole season.
